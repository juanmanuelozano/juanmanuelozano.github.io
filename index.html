About

I am an economist and a Government and Public Affairs graduate from Universidad de los Andes, where I am completing an M.A. in Economics. My research focuses in the economics of crime and security: prisons and incarceration, case processing times in criminal proceedings, illegal markets and illicit crops, and urban violence. I also work on labor markets and the quality of official statistics.

I like questions where the answer depends on how an institution actually works, and I spend most of my time building the data needed to answer them — crime and judicial records, household surveys, administrative registries — and then arguing about what those data can and cannot support. I plan to pursue a Ph.D. in Economics.

Outside of research I co-created Al Margen, a podcast with Colombian macroeconomists, and I am on the board of the graduate research seminars at the Faculty of Economics. I am a lifelong football player, a guitarist, and a slow but stubborn student of Greek.

I work in Stata, R and Python, and use LaTeX and ArcGIS Pro.
