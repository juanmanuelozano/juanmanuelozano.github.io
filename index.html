About

I am an economist and a Government and Public Affairs graduate from Universidad de los Andes, where I am completing an M.A. in Economics (PEG). I work as a research assistant at CEDE, with Daniel Mejía on crime, security and the criminal justice system, and with Hernando Zuleta on labor markets and official statistics.

Most of my work starts with data that does not yet exist in usable form: incident-level crime and judicial records, household surveys, social security registries. I build those datasets, georeference them, and take them to questions about how policy actually changes behavior — using applied causal inference, spatial analysis and, where it helps, machine learning. I care as much about the mechanism behind an estimate as about the estimate itself, which is probably what my second degree in public affairs left me with.

I intend to pursue a Ph.D. in Economics.
