About

I am an economist and a government and public affairs graduate from Universidad de los Andes, where I am completing an M.A. in Economics. My research focuses on the economics of crime and security: prisons and incarceration, case processing times in criminal proceedings, illegal markets and illicit crops, and urban violence. I also work on labor markets and the quality of official statistics.

Macroeconomics and monetary policy have been a lasting interest since my undergraduate years. That passion is the reason I teach macroeconomics and why I co-created Al Margen, a podcast with Colombian macroeconomists.

I like questions where the answer depends on how an institution actually works, and I spend most of my time building the data needed to answer them (crime and judicial records, household surveys, administrative registries) and then arguing about what those data can and cannot support. I plan to pursue a Ph.D. in Economics.

Outside of research I sit on the board of the graduate research seminars at the Faculty of Economics. I am a lifelong football player, a guitarist, and a slow but stubborn student of Greek.

Tools. Stata, R and Python. LaTeX and ArcGIS Pro.
